Goal Name: Advance Cryptographic Architecture Standards and Threat Modeling Excellence
Objective:
Lead the creation and refinement of firmwide cryptographic architecture controls and threat models supporting tokenization, HSMs, and emerging confidential computing initiatives. Ensure architectural patterns are well-defined, risk-aware, and ready for enterprise adoption.

Success Criteria:

Complete or direct the completion of at least two major control procedure or threat-modeling initiatives supporting cryptography or confidential computing.

Ensure all deliverables align to NIST SP 800-57, PCI PIN, and internal cryptographic control objectives.

Deliver high-quality documentation reviewed and accepted by senior architecture and compliance stakeholders.

Contribute to enterprise readiness for confidential computing workloads and key-management modernization.

Timeline:
January – December 2025

Results:
Throughout 2025, I guided the cybersecurity architecture team through several key cryptographic initiatives that advanced our firm’s maturity in secure key management and confidential computing readiness.

I directed and reviewed the ongoing Confidential Computing Control Procedure, establishing its structure, scope, and relationship to existing encryption and key-management frameworks. This deliverable sets the foundation for consistent use of secure enclaves and workload isolation within AWS environments.

I provided architectural oversight and governance input for the HSM and Tokenization Security Control Procedure, ensuring that it accurately represented lifecycle management practices aligned to NIST and PCI PIN. My review and feedback sessions helped raise the document’s clarity and precision before enterprise publication.

I personally developed and documented two major threat models: one for a Hold-Your-Own-Key (HYOK) solution using AWS KMS, and another for the Thales CipherTrust platform. These analyses detailed key-wrapping techniques, boundaries of key ownership, and potential attack surfaces for both on-premise and cloud key-management implementations.

The resulting outputs directly influenced cryptographic design patterns across multiple lines of business, enabling teams to make better-informed design decisions and reducing ambiguity around key isolation and control boundaries.
